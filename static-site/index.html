The STIEM Framework

Five forces. Assessed together, never in isolation.

The STIEM Framework is Stiemfield's proprietary methodology for diagnosing and architecting organisational convergence. It is built on a simple premise: transformation fails when its five critical forces are managed in isolation.

S

Strategy

The organisation's stated direction, priorities and resource allocation.

Does the organisation have a clear, communicated strategy that guides decision-making?

Failure modeStrategy exists on paper but does not influence daily decisions or resource allocation.

T

Technology

The systems, tools and platforms used to execute work.

Is technology deployed, adopted and integrated into daily workflows?

Failure modeTechnology deployed but bypassed; systems management never reorganised around.

I

Innovation

The organisation's capacity to experiment, learn and scale new ideas.

Do pilots scale into operations?

Failure modeInnovation theatre — many pilots, few scaled outcomes.

E

Execution

The organisation's ability to deliver on its commitments.

Are projects delivered on time, on budget and with intended impact?

Failure modeThe strategy–execution gap; plans that never translate into results.

M

Management

The leadership, governance and organisational capacity to sustain change.

Is there aligned leadership that builds lasting capacity?

Failure modeLeadership turnover, unclear accountability, or management resistance to change.

The multiplicative principleConvergence Score = Strategy × Technology × Innovation × Execution × Management

The five forces are not additive. They are multiplicative. If any one force is weak, the entire system underperforms.

A strong strategy with weak execution produces nothing. Strong technology with weak management produces bypassed systems. Strong innovation with weak strategy produces scattered pilots. This is why Stiemfield assesses all five forces together — never in isolation.

How we work

Diagnosis first. Then architecture.

Architecture on opinion is the failure we exist to correct. Every engagement begins with evidence, and every recommendation is sequenced.

The diagnostic process

  1. Evidence collection — interviews, document review, system data.
  2. Force scoring — each force scored on a 1–5 scale.
  3. Connection mapping — how well each force links to the others.
  4. Evidence Reliability rating — how strong is the evidence behind each score?
  5. Convergence Map — visual and narrative summary of findings.
  6. Roadmap — prioritised recommendations for closing gaps.

The architectural process

  1. Vision alignment — confirm strategic direction.
  2. Information flow design — map how information moves between forces.
  3. System architecture — design the technology that supports information flow.
  4. Governance design — define decision rights, accountability and reporting.
  5. Instrumentation — build metrics that track convergence on an ongoing basis.
  6. Implementation roadmap — sequence the build.

Delivered inside Fieldforce and Fieldpartner rather than sold separately. Architecture is not a product you buy once; it is how implementation is done.

How the ladder sequences

Engagements are ordered by the arithmetic, not by budget.

  • Diagnosis precedes delivery. Fieldlab, Fieldforce and Fieldpartner all require a Fieldscan within the prior twelve months.
  • A Snapshot credits toward a Fieldscan if commissioned within 90 days, so the low-risk entry point costs the client nothing to take.
  • Delivery follows the weakest force. If Innovation scores lowest the next engagement is Fieldlab; if Execution, Fieldforce; if Management, Fieldpartner. We will say so even when a larger engagement would earn more.
  • Fieldpartner follows delivery. It sustains a gain; it cannot create one.
  • Two concurrent Fieldscans, maximum. Each is authored by the Founding Partner.

Our story

A career that began in the field.

Asar Paul T

Founding Partner

Stiemfield Global Convergence Limited was founded by Asar Paul T, a business management graduate whose career began not in a boardroom, but in the field. During his National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) year in Osun State, he served as State Project Manager for Digital Literacy for All — a community development service initiative under NITDA's DL4ALL programme. In that role he coordinated digital literacy training across communities, working with local leaders, schools and grassroots organisations to deliver practical technology skills to Nigerians who had been left behind by the digital economy.

That experience was formative. It revealed that the biggest barrier to transformation was never technology. It was the failure to connect strategy, technology, innovation, execution and management into a single, coherent system. Stiemfield Global Convergence Limited was founded to solve that problem — first in Africa, and now for the world.
